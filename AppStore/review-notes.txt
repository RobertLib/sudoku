The first launch opens a short Welcome sheet listing what the app offers; Start Playing at its bottom closes it, and it is not shown again. No account or sign-in is required, and there is nothing to unlock — tap New Game, pick a level, and the whole app is available on the first launch.

There are no in-app purchases and no advertising. Nothing in the app is for sale and no feature is limited by a daily quota or a timer. The hints — the app's main feature, which explain each logical step with highlighted cells and drawn chains — are free and unlimited like the rest of it.

NO GAMBLING OF ANY KIND. Sudoku is a logic puzzle. Nothing is wagered, nothing pays out, there are no loot boxes or random rewards, and the app has no StoreKit code and no networking code, so it cannot transact with anything. The Chance-Based Activities section of the age rating questionnaire is answered None, and the Gambling declaration in App Information is No.

Fully offline. The puzzle generator, the solver and the hint engine are the app's own and run on the device; there are no packages, no third-party SDKs and no network requests of any kind. The daily challenge needs no server either: each day's puzzle is generated from the date, so it is the same on every device. The sounds are synthesized at launch, so the app ships with no audio files. Settings are stored in UserDefaults (declared in sudoku/PrivacyInfo.xcprivacy, reason CA92.1); unfinished games, results, statistics, daily challenge results and achievements are JSON files in the app's Application Support directory, and a few puzzles generated in advance are kept in Caches. Nothing leaves the device.

Clipboard: the Custom Puzzle screen (Home → Custom Puzzle) lets the player paste a puzzle copied from elsewhere. It uses the system PasteButton, so the clipboard is read only when the player taps it, and the app never reads the clipboard on its own.

Third-party content: none. The puzzles are generated by the app, the hint explanations and the technique guide are original, and the icon, the interface and the sounds are the app's own.

How to play: tap New Game and choose Easy, Medium, Hard, Expert or Master. Select a cell and tap a number to fill it; Notes switches the number pad to pencil marks. Hint (the light bulb under the board) first points to the area to look at; Show Me then explains the exact step, and Apply makes it. The menu (•••) at the top right holds Redo, Auto Notes, Clear All Notes, Reveal Selected Cell, Restart and Show Solution, plus Check Board when mistake checking in Settings is set to Rule Conflicts or Off (with the default, Instant, wrong numbers already turn red). The Daily Challenge card on the home screen opens today's puzzle and the calendar of past ones. Statistics, Achievements, Custom Puzzle and How to Play are the four tiles below it, and the gear at the top right opens Settings (mistake checking, input mode, highlighting, appearance, theme colors, number font, sounds and haptics).

Accessibility: every cell is a VoiceOver element that says its position, its number or notes and whether it is wrong, and every control has a label, so a whole game can be played by ear. Dynamic Type is supported (around the board, text stops at the first accessibility size, because the board itself cannot grow), and Reduce Motion is honored.

Localization: English and Czech, switched by the device language. Settings → Language opens the app's page in the iOS Settings app, where the language can be changed for this app alone once the device has more than one preferred language; the app has no language switch of its own.

The app supports iPhone and iPad, in portrait and landscape, and keyboard shortcuts with a hardware keyboard.
